關於我 About Phoenix

繁體中文
中文系及性別研究系畢業，通曉粵語、國語、英文及日文。醉心性別文學及女性主義，現時多創作散文及詩，偶有文化及文學評論見諸各大報刊。

日前完成以英文書寫的碩士論文，題目是重構少女年華﹕日本少女漫畫NANA中的性別、親密關係及浪漫。

本研究以日本少女漫畫《NANA》為研究對象，探討這部最具代表性和影響力的當代少女漫畫之一如何呈現女性形象和性別角色。過去研究普遍認為，少女漫畫傾向強化傳統的浪漫敘事和性別刻板印象。然而，《NANA》透過主角Nana與Hachi之間的友誼與愛情關係，展現了更為複雜和反思性的女性主體性。本研究採用話語分析和視覺分析方法，並結合女性主義批判框架，檢視漫畫的視覺圖像、內心獨白和敘事內容，分析《NANA》如何在女性主義脈絡下打破傳統少女漫畫的性別模式。研究發現，《NANA》透過描繪女性的性別特質、非規範的親密友誼模式以及異性戀父權制愛情體系下的性別期待，挑戰了以愛為中心的少女漫畫敘事框架，呈現了更為真實的女性體驗。這項研究的意義在於，透過結合東方和西方女性主義理論，《NANA》的分析提出了關於女性身分和關係的新想像和可能性，為研究日本流行文化中的女性主體性提供了寶貴的見解。

同時近年已撰寫完成關於離散議題、身體政治及殘疾文學研究等內容，正準備刊登。
English
A writer and researcher with degrees in Chinese Literature and Gender Studies, Phoenix is multilingual in Cantonese, Mandarin, English, and Japanese. Passionate about gender and queer literature, she is currently working on her own literary projects exploring gender topics. Her primary creative focus lies in prose and poetry, alongside cultural and literary reviews published in major newspapers and periodicals.

In September 2026, She finished her Master’s thesis in English, Topic is Reconstructing the Myth of Girl: Gender, Intimacy and Romance in a Japanese Shōjo Manga NANA

This study takes a Japanese Shōjo manga NANA, as its research subject, exploring how one of the most representative and influential contemporary Shōjo manga presents female images and gender roles. Previous research has generally concluded that Shōjo manga tends to reinforce traditional romantic narratives and gender stereotypes. However, NANA, through the friendship and romantic relationship between the protagonists Nana and Hachi, showcases a more complex and reflective female subjectivity. This study employs discourse and visual analysis, combined with a feminist critical approach framework, to examine the manga visual image, inner dialogue and narrative content, analysing how NANA deconstrusts the gender formula of traditional Shōjo manga through feminist context. The study finds that NANA, by depicting female gender traits, non-normative friendship models of intimacy and gender expectations under a heteropatriarchal system of love, challenges the romance-centric narrative framework of Shōjo manga, presenting a more realistic portrayal of female experiences. The significance of this study lies in the fact that, by combining Eastern and Western feminist theories, the analysis of NANA presents new imaginations and possibilities regarding female identity and relationships, offering valuable insights for the study of female subjectivity in Japanese popular culture.

Meanwhile, she has recently completed writings on diaspora issues, body politics, and literature on disability, which are currently being prepared for publication.
